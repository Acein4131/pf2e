# The Drelev Campaign — A Days of War Kit

*For Kingmaker Chapter 7, "Blood for Blood." PCs level 10–11, kingdom level about 10.*

Baron Hannis Drelev has declared war. Rather than wait for Pitax or the Tiger
Lords to do his work, the craven baron spends Irovetti's silver on mercenaries,
borrows hill giants and Tiger Lord riders, and sets out to bleed the PCs' western
border before they can march on him. This kit turns that declaration into a
**campaign of roughly 15–30 days**, played with the rules in [`rules.md`](rules.md).
It ends with Fort Drelev liberated (or not), feeding straight into the module's
Liberation Points and the fight in Drelev Keep.

Page numbers refer to the Kingmaker Adventure Path hardcover.

---

## 1. Where This Fits

- **When Drelev declares war.** In the module this follows the Battle for Tatzlford
  (Part 1, pp. 288–299) and Kisandra Numesti's plea (p. 299). If your PCs never
  reached Tatzlford, start here anyway; Kisandra can find them at the capital instead.
- **Replaces** the gap between Part 1 and Part 2, which the module expects you to fill
  with Kingdom turns. **Part 2** (Fort Drelev and Drelev Keep, pp. 300–317) becomes the
  campaign's climax rather than its opening.
- **Leave Part 3** (Armag's Tomb) **after the war.** Armag is busy delving his
  ancestor's tomb and only lends Drelev riders. The campaign seeds Part 3 (Windchaser,
  the Tiger Lord hills, Tamary Numesti) without resolving it.

## 2. The Theater

This kit assumes the kingdom described below. Adjust it if yours differs.

- The **capital** is at the Stag Lord's old fort on the Tuskwater (zone 3).
- The kingdom's hexes are mostly in the **Greenbelt** and around the Tuskwater. It
  has **barely reached into the Narlmarches**.
- The PCs **already command the bog striders of Tok-Nikrat** (HT12).
- The PCs once ruled the **M'botuu boggards** (HT6) but **handed leadership to
  Drelev**. The boggards still regard the PCs as their true leaders, but Drelev's
  overseer holds them unless a PC goes to M'botuu in person.

| Zone / Site | Role in the war |
| --- | --- |
| **The capital and the Greenbelt** (zones 2–3) | Your heartland and supply base. Drelev's raiders hit the kingdom's **westernmost hexes** first: whatever Friendly hexes lie closest to the Narlmarches and the Slough. |
| **Tatzlford** (GB16) | Only matters if it's yours. If it is, it's your forward settlement and Drelev's first siege target. If not, it's a neutral town Drelev may bully or occupy. |
| **The Narlmarches** (zone 5) | Mostly **Wild** hexes between you and the Slough. Thick forest (difficult terrain), no supply, and hard on armies. Expect to build **depots** to cross it, and expect raiders to hunt those depots. |
| **Hooktongue Slough** (zone 10, pp. 94–103) | Greater difficult swamp. **This is the PCs' natural road to Drelev.** Their bog striders know it, and M'botuu, if reclaimed, becomes a forward base in its heart. Without guides it's slow and dangerous. |
| **Tok-Nikrat** (HT12) | Your bog striders' home. It's a supply source for armies the striders guide, and a target for Drelev's boggards (the two peoples are old enemies). |
| **M'botuu** (HT6) | The boggard village, **held by Drelev's overseer** at the start. If a PC reclaims it in person (Mission 3), it becomes a friendly supply source in the Slough and its Frog Riders return to the PCs. |
| **Wyvernstone Bridge** (HT1) | The northern crossing of the East Sellen. **It's off the PCs' route.** It only matters if one of the hooks in §6 (Mission 1) makes it matter. |
| **Drelev plains** (zone 11, pp. 103–105) | Open grassland north and west of the Slough, and fast ground for cavalry. Contains Speartooth's Den (DR1), the Wild Horses (DR3), and the Desperate Refugees (DR4). |
| **Drelev's Forward Camp** (new) | Place it on the plains or at the Slough's edge, **2–3 hexes from Fort Drelev, facing M'botuu**. It's the depot for Drelev's field armies. |
| **Fort Drelev** (DR2) | The baron's seat on Lake Hooktongue (pp. 300–317). |
| **Tiger Lord hills** (zone 12) | West of Drelev. The Outriders ride in from here. |

> **The likely road to war:** capital → west through the Greenbelt → across the
> Narlmarches on a chain of depots → into the Slough with the bog striders →
> M'botuu (if reclaimed) → the Forward Camp → Fort Drelev. Drelev's best strategy is to
> raid that chain and use the boggards to make the Slough deadly.

### The Slough Takes Its Toll

Hooktongue Slough doesn't just slow armies down. It grinds them apart: fever,
leeches, rotting boots, spoiled stores, and things in the water. These rules apply to
**every army, on either side**, in a swamp hex of zone 10 (not the drier hexes in the
zone's northeast).

**In battle.** Every war encounter fought in a Slough hex uses the book's **Difficult
Terrain** battlefield feature (Appendix 3): −2 circumstance penalty to Maneuver checks.
Armies with Amphibious, Water Stride, or Swamp Dwellers ignore it, as their stat blocks
say.

**The Slough Check (each Night).** At Night, after supply, every army that ends the
day in a Slough hex attempts a **Maneuver check** (the Maneuver statistic is for
"unusual physical dangers," Appendix 3) against the **Slough DC**:

> **Slough DC = 23, +1 for each consecutive Night after the third** that the army has
> spent in the Slough (4th Night DC 24, 5th DC 25, and so on).

The count of consecutive Nights resets once the army spends a Night outside the Slough
or **inside a friendly Slough settlement**: Tok-Nikrat (with the Scouts present) or a
reclaimed M'botuu. An army camped in a friendly Slough settlement doesn't roll at all.

| Modifier | Bonus/penalty |
| --- | --- |
| The army benefits from *Swamp Guides* (your bog striders, or the Hooktongue Raiders' boggards) | +2 circumstance |
| The army is in a hex with a **causeway** (below) | +2 circumstance (if guides also apply, the total is +3) |
| The army is **unsupplied** tonight | −2 circumstance |
| **Cavalry** (horses founder and sicken) | −2 circumstance |
| **Siege** (engines sink) | −4 circumstance |
| The army has Amphibious, Water Stride, or Swamp Dwellers (Frog Riders, Tok-Nikrat Scouts, lizardfolk) | no check needed |

- **Critical Success** The army weathers the night. If it's weary, it reduces weary
  by 1 even if it moved today.
- **Success** No effect.
- **Failure** Roll once on the **Slough Hazards** table.
- **Critical Failure** Roll twice on the Slough Hazards table (reroll duplicates), and
  increase weary by 1.

**Slough Hazards (d8)**

| d8 | Hazard | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Marsh fever** | Weary +1. The fever lingers: the army can't reduce weary by resting until it has spent a Night outside the Slough or in a friendly Slough settlement. A **Recover Army** Command for weary (Defense, or Arts at expert, per Appendix 3) also ends the fever. |
| 2 | **Fever spreads** | As Marsh fever. Also, one other army of the same side in the same hex must attempt a Slough check against the same DC immediately. |
| 3 | **The sucking mire** | Mired 2 until the end of tomorrow's March or Dusk phase, so the army will likely lose its movement tomorrow. Siege armies also take 1 damage as an engine sinks. |
| 4 | **Spoiled stores** | Damp and rot ruin its food and bowstrings. The army is **unsupplied tonight** even if a supply source is in range (pay 1 WC or weary +1), and an army with ranged Strikes starts its next war encounter with 2 fewer shots. |
| 5 | **Leeches and rot** | Feet rot, armor rusts, and soldiers sicken. Weary +1. |
| 6 | **Will-o'-wisps** | Ghost lights draw sentries into the deep water, and some never come back. Shaken +1. |
| 7 | **Something in the water** | A predator from the zone 10 table (p. 95) hits the camp. The army takes 1 damage. If any PC is in the hex, the PCs can fight that encounter instead. If they win, the army takes no damage. |
| 8 | **Boggard war-party** | While Drelev holds M'botuu, boggards raid the camp: 1 damage, and the army becomes shaken 1. If the PCs have **reclaimed M'botuu**, the boggards bring frogs and eels instead: the army is supplied tonight and reduces weary by 1. (Drelev's armies always take the bad version once M'botuu is reclaimed.) |

**Lake Hooktongue.** An army that **critically fails** a Slough check in a hex bordering
Lake Hooktongue (HT7) wakes **Hooktongue** himself (HT7, p. 97). The army takes 2 damage, and
the water orm becomes a roaming threat on the lake for the rest of the war. This is a
dramatic event; the PCs may want to deal with it as an encounter.

**Lay a Causeway (Command).** *Engineering or Wilderness*, DC = Control DC, **1 WC**,
in person or by courier. Choose a Slough hex where one of your armies is present. Your
soldiers lay brush-and-log causeways and mark the safe ground.
- **Critical Success** As success, and it costs no WC.
- **Success** For the rest of the war, the hex has a causeway. Your armies get +2 on
  Slough checks there (enemy armies don't; they don't know the markings), and it counts
  as difficult terrain instead of greater difficult for your armies.
- **Failure** The work sinks. The WC is spent.
- **Critical Failure** As failure, and the working army becomes weary 1.

> **Why this matters.** The Slough is the PCs' road to Drelev. Each night there is a
> real cost, so the war becomes a question of how fast they can cross, where they can
> rest, and how much they're willing to spend on causeways, guides, and reclaiming
> M'botuu. It also cuts both ways: if Drelev's field armies chase the PCs into the
> swamp, they suffer just as much, and the PCs' guides give them the edge.

## 3. Setup (Day 0)

1. **Record the kingdom:** level, Control DC, skill modifiers, Unrest, Ruins,
   Fame/Infamy, stored Commodities, settlements, and armies.
2. **Mobilization check** → starting War Chest (`rules.md` §3).
3. **Starting Strain** = current Unrest ÷ 2 (rounded down).
4. **Your armies:** place any armies the kingdom already has.
   - The **Tok-Nikrat Scouts** (the bog strider army, Army 10 skirmisher, Appendix 3 p. 573) start at
     Tok-Nikrat. See *Swamp Guides* below.
   - If the PCs kept the **Tatzlford Town Guard** (Army 7) and **Narlmarch Hunters**
     (Army 6) after Part 1 (p. 296), they are in Tatzlford. Those two armies don't
     scale with kingdom level unless the PCs paid to keep them on, as the book
     describes.
5. **Supply sources:** your settlements, plus **Tok-Nikrat**. Tok-Nikrat supplies only
   armies that are escorted by, or in the same hex as, the Tok-Nikrat Scouts. The
   striders won't feed strangers.
6. **Enemy armies:** place them as shown in §4.
7. **War Clock** = 0. If you ran the Battle for Tatzlford, adjust the Clock by how it
   went. If you skipped Tatzlford, use the "Ameon killed" row, or the "escaped" row
   if you want Ameon around as a recurring enemy.

**Swamp Guides (your bog striders).** Any of your armies that starts the day in the same
hex as the Tok-Nikrat Scouts, or escorts them, treats Hooktongue Slough as **difficult**
terrain instead of greater difficult and may Forage in swamp hexes. Losing the Scouts
loses this benefit, so Drelev will hunt them.

| Tatzlford outcome | Adjustments |
| --- | --- |
| Successful Victory, Ameon Trask **captured** | Clock 0. If Ameon is made helpful (p. 299), he gives up the whole enemy order of battle: on Day 1 the GM reveals every enemy army's location. |
| Successful Victory, Ameon **killed** | Clock 0. |
| Victory, Ameon **escaped** | Clock 2. Ameon commands the Pitaxian Free Company (see §4). |
| Costly Victory | Clock 2, Strain +1. |
| Total Defeat | Clock 4, Strain +3. Tatzlford starts **enemy-held**, garrisoned by any surviving Drelev Irregulars and Troll Marauders (p. 297). Retaking it comes first. |

### Allied Relief from Restov

Lady **Jamandi Aldori** (p. 599) organized the push into the Stolen Lands, and Restov's
swordlords chartered the PCs. A Brevic baron making war on their charter-holders is an
insult the swordlords won't ignore. Jamandi sends a company of Aldori duelists south.

- They won't Strike the **Drelev Conscripts**. They engage them only to make them rout.
- If the kingdom uses **Martial Law**, or any of your armies **defeats** the Conscripts,

**Restov's Purse** (unique) Restov pays its own. The Company costs nothing on Pay Day

**Commander** **Swordlord Teodor Ruskin**, one of Jamandi's protégés (an invented NPC).

Commander, he can duel the enemy commander alongside them.

#### How they arrive

- **When:** they appear at the **north edge of the map on the East Sellen road** on
  **Day 7**. The road meets the river about 40 miles, roughly 3 hexes, north of
  Wyvernstone Bridge (p. 51). Skirmishers move 2 activities per day.
- **Write to Restov** (once per war; Statecraft or Politics; Control DC). A PC sends an
  urgent appeal to Jamandi.
  - **Critical Success** The Company arrives 3 days earlier.
  - **Success** It arrives 1 day earlier.
  - **Failure** No change.
  - **Critical Failure** The appeal reads as panic. Restov sends them anyway, but they
    arrive 2 days later.
- **The road runs over Wyvernstone Bridge.** The Company has no swamp guides and won't
  bring Restov's pride through the Slough. **This is the PCs' reason to go north
  (Mission 1).**
  - Drelev's spies spot the Company the day it appears. At the next Dusk, unless Drelev
    already has an army at the bridge, the **Pitaxian Free Company** marches to hold
    the bridge behind field works (trench: AC 15, HP 1).
  - If the bridge is held by Drelev when the Company arrives, the Company attacks.
    Resolve it as a Clash, or as a full War encounter if the PCs are there.
  - If the bridge is **broken**, the Company detours to an upriver crossing and arrives
    **5 days later, weary 1**.
- **Once across,** the Company joins the PCs' armies, takes orders like any of them, and
  draws supply normally.

#### After the war

- **The Company survives:** at the Reckoning, gain **1 Fame**. Restov's swordlords now

### Allied Army: The Lodovka River Fleet

House **Lodovka** has worked the waters of the Lake of Mists and Veils since before
Brevoy existed. Their motto is "The Waters, Our Fields" (Chapter 1, p. 12). When word
reaches the north that a Brevic baron has shut the East Sellen trade route and made war
on Restov's charter-holders, the house sends **Swin Lodovka** south with a flotilla of
armed keelboats and river galleys.

**THE LODOVKA RIVER FLEET** — ARMY 11
*N Infantry (naval), allied specialized army*
**Scouting** +21; **Recruitment DC** 33 (+5 adjustment); **Consumption** 2
**Description** A dozen shallow-draft longships and keelboats crewed by Lodovka
rivermen, marines, and boarding crews. Their decks bristle with mounted ballistae and
crossbows, and their prows are carved with the house's sea-serpent. They fight from the
water, where landlocked armies can't reach them, and they come ashore only when they
choose to.
**AC** 31; **Maneuver** +18 (low); **Morale** +24 (high)
**HP** 6 (RT 3)
**Melee** boarding axes and cutlasses +24
**Ranged** deck ballistae and crossbows +24 (7 shots)
**Tactics** Increased Ammunition, Hold the Line
**Waterborne** (unique) The fleet moves only along navigable water: the East Sellen,
Lake Hooktongue, and the Slough's channels. It can't enter a hex without a river or
lake.
- **River or lake hexes:** 3 activities per day, treating them as open terrain.
  Downstream on the East Sellen, it gets 4 activities.
- **Slough channels:** difficult terrain. Lodovka pilots read water the way bog

choose to engage a shore army by putting marines ashore, but then it loses this benefit
until the next war encounter.

**Ballista Decks** (unique tactic) The fleet can use Battle with its ranged Strike to
damage **fortifications that border water**, as a siege army can. Fort Drelev qualifies:
it has two water borders and two piers on Lake Hooktongue (DR2, p. 104). This is the PCs'
best way to crack the fort without dragging siege engines through the swamp.

**Floating Supply** (unique) The fleet is its own supply source, but only for itself and
any army it carries. Its holds are full of salt fish and Brevic grain.

#### Swin Lodovka

**Commander** **Swin Lodovka**, captain of the flotilla and a scion of House Lodovka. Use
a level 12–13 captain or pirate NPC, or adapt Ameon Trask (p. 298) with the elite
adjustments, a swim speed of 25 feet, and Athletics in place of Acrobatics as his best
skill. Lodovka family members are trained in Athletics and Fishing Lore and have the
Underwater Marauder feat (p. 12).

When Swin is present for Strike the Commander, he leads boarding actions personally.
If he is killed or captured, the fleet becomes shaken 2 and retreats 1 hex upriver at
the next Dusk.

#### Getting the fleet into the war

- **When:** the fleet appears at the **north map edge on the East Sellen** on **Day 10**
  by default. Set your own day if you prefer, or let the PCs send word north to bring it
  sooner, the same way as Write to Restov.
- **Wyvernstone gorge:** the river runs beneath Wyvernstone Bridge (HT1) on its way
  south.
  - **Drelev army holds the bridge:** the fleet must run the gorge under falling rocks
    and arrows. It attempts a Maneuver check against that army's Standard DC. On a
    failure it takes 1 damage, and on a critical failure it takes 2 damage and loses a
    day.
  - **Bridge broken:** the rubble chokes the gorge, and **the fleet can't pass** until
    the channel is cleared. Clearing it takes 3 days of work by the fleet plus a
    successful Engineering Command. Breaking the bridge now blocks both Restov's road
    and Lodovka's river. The players should weigh that.
- **After the gorge,** the river carries the fleet into the Slough and on to Lake
  Hooktongue, right up to Fort Drelev's piers.
- **Hooktongue.** Each Night the fleet spends on Lake Hooktongue, roll a d20. On a 1, the
  water orm Hooktongue (HT7, p. 97) rises beneath the boats. The fleet takes 2 damage,
  and Hooktongue becomes a roaming threat if it wasn't already.

#### After the war

House Lodovka wants payment, though not in coin. At the Reckoning, Swin asks the PCs to
promise **Lodovka trading rights on the East Sellen and Lake Hooktongue**, including
berths at Fort Drelev's piers if the PCs take it.
- **Grant it:** gain 1 Fame. The river trade route reopens under Lodovka's protection.
  Brevoy's other houses will notice that the PCs have a Lodovka friend.
- **Refuse:** Lodovka sails home. Increase Unrest by 1 as the river merchants complain,
  and House Lodovka remembers it.

> **Running the fleet as an enemy instead.** The stat block works unchanged if House
> Lodovka sides with Drelev, for example because he promised them the river trade. Put
> the fleet on the East Sellen on Day 10 and have it sail to Lake Hooktongue to garrison
> Fort Drelev's piers. *The Waters, Our Fields* then makes the lakefront nearly
> unassailable, and *Ferry* lets Drelev move an army across the Slough quickly. That's a
> strong reason for the PCs to break or hold Wyvernstone Bridge. Use the Restov
> duelists, or the Tok-Nikrat Scouts with *Water Stride*, to fight it.

## 6. Missions

Missions are where the party's hexploration and encounters drive the war. Each is a
normal adventuring session. **(Hope)** marks missions that reduce Strain when
completed.

**Banked Liberation Points:** the campaign can bank **at most 10 LP** toward Fort
Drelev's 20 (p. 301). Whispers Behind the Walls can contribute **at most 6** of those.

### Mission 1 — The Wyvernstone Crossing (HT1) · Optional
The bridge is far north of the PCs' road through the Slough. **Don't push it on them.**
It's guarded only by a small Drelev outpost: a sergeant and 4 Drelev guards (p. 304),
not an army. It becomes worth the detour if one of these reasons comes up:

- **The Outriders' road.** The Tiger Lord Outriders cross here to raid your heartland
  (§4). If their raids are hurting, holding or breaking the bridge forces them through
  the Slough as greater difficult terrain, which effectively ends their raids on your
  core hexes.
- **Restov's relief.** The Swordlords' Company comes down the East Sellen road and must
  cross here (§4, *Allied Relief from Restov*). Once Drelev sends the Free Company to
  hold the bridge against them, the PCs can storm it from the south while the duelists
  attack from the north. That's a two-front named battle in which the PCs' armies and
  the Company fight together. Breaking the bridge to stop the Tiger Lords also delays
  Restov by 5 days, so the two hooks pull against each other.
- **Drelev's escape route.** If Drelev reaches **Entrenched** posture (§4), he quietly
  readies a flight north to Brevoy. If the bridge is open when the PCs finally corner
  him, he may flee instead of fighting in his keep. That's a reason to hold it late in
  the war.

**Doing it:**
- **Take it:** the party storms the outpost (a Moderate 11 encounter). Then station
  any army there with a **Hold** order, or it slips back into Drelev's hands at the next
  Dusk an enemy army passes.
- **Break it:** a DC 30 Engineering check by a PC (or Crafting with explosives or
  magic), taking a full day. The bridge is a 200-year-old landmark, so breaking it is
  permanent. It also cuts the old East Sellen trade road, which may have kingdom
  consequences of your choosing after the war.
- **Reward:** War Clock −1, plus whichever hook made it worth the trip.

### Mission 2 — Burn the Forward Camp (Drelev plains)
The Stonethrowers (and the Troll Marauders, if alive) guard Drelev's depot.
- **Infiltration:** DC 28 Stealth to approach (Avoid Notice). Inside, the party faces
  the giants' elite chieftain and a few hill giants (around Severe 11) while setting fires.
- **Battle option:** defeat the garrison armies, and the camp falls as well.
- **Reward:** the depot is destroyed, War Clock −2, and +3 WC. Enemy field armies more
  than 2 hexes from Fort Drelev become unsupplied.

### Mission 3 — The Kings Return to M'botuu (HT6) · (Hope: Strain −1)
The boggards still call the PCs their kings, but only the PCs' presence can break the
overseer's hold. **At least one PC must personally go to M'botuu.** No Command or
courier can do this.
- **Getting there:** M'botuu is deep in the Slough and patrolled by the Frog Riders
  while Drelev holds them. The bog striders can guide the party, but the two peoples
  hate each other. Bringing the Tok-Nikrat Scouts **into** M'botuu worsens the
  boggards' attitude by one step. Leaving them at the village's edge is wiser.
- **At the village:** the overseer has the boggards' ear, but not their hearts. Run it
  as a social and combat scene. Possible approaches:
  - Publicly reclaim the throne with Intimidation or Diplomacy against the overseer
    (use the boggard Chapter 2 Part 9 material, p. 154, for the village's leaders).
  - Challenge the overseer, or their boggard champion, to single combat. Boggards
    respect strength.
  - Expose Drelev's bad faith, for example with proof that he's spending boggard lives
    as Slough guides for bandits. Use Society or Deception, then Diplomacy.
- **If it goes badly,** the overseer orders the boggards to attack. Every PC success
  above can still flip individual boggards mid-fight.
- **Reward:**
  - The **M'botuu Frog Riders** rejoin the PCs (see *Old Loyalties*).
  - **M'botuu becomes a friendly supply source** in the Slough, and a base the
    armies can rest in.
  - The **Hooktongue Raiders lose Swamp Guides**, and Daily Event 18 stops.
  - Drelev learns of it. War Clock +1, but Drelev must now attack the Slough
    through hostile swamp.
- **Optional consequence:** if the PCs want boggards and bog striders fighting side by
  side, require a Statecraft Command (Control DC) at M'botuu or Tok-Nikrat to broker a
  wartime truce. Until then, the two armies can't end a day in the same hex.

### Mission 4 — Windchaser (DR3)
Make the awakened stallion friendly (p. 105).
- **Reward:** +2 circumstance bonus to Scout the Front in zones 11 and 12. Once during
  the war, Windchaser leads one army along hidden trails: it moves up to 3 open hexes in
  a single day and can't be intercepted by Patrols. If made helpful, Windchaser also
  reveals the Tiger Lords' hidden valley, a Part 3 lead.

### Mission 5 — The Desperate Refugees (DR4) · (Hope: Strain −2)
Escort the refugees (p. 105) to any friendly settlement, or to M'botuu if it has been
reclaimed, where the boggards will shelter them (grudgingly) until the war ends. Your
heartland is a long walk away, so M'botuu is usually the practical choice. They move
as infantry (1 activity per day) and the Hooktongue Raiders hunt them. If raiders end Dusk in their
hex without a friendly army there, the escort fails.
- **Reward:** Strain −2, Unrest −2 (as written, applied immediately), and +1 banked LP
  (their stories reach Fort Drelev). The free Trade Shop from the book is available
  when Kingdom turns resume.

### Mission 6 — Speartooth (DR1)
Speartooth now fights for Drelev as **Speartooth's Pride** (§4). The PCs can face it in
a battle with Strike the Commander, or hunt the beast directly. Track it to its den
(DR1, p. 104) at night while the Pride is being fed, or lure it out with bait away from
the rest of the pack (a Severe 14 encounter on its own, so be careful).
- **Reward for killing Speartooth:** the Pride is destroyed (War Clock −2, as for any
  destroyed army). The Tiger Lords revere Speartooth, and its death is a dire omen:
  every Tiger Lord army immediately attempts a DC 28 Morale check and **leaves the
  campaign** on a failure. Drelev's guards are quietly relieved, so bank +1 LP. The
  2,000 gp bounty is paid as written.
- **Reward for freeing it instead** (breaking the collar or dealing with Imeckus): the
  Pride leaves Drelev without the Tiger Lords' Morale check, since their god-beast still
  lives. Speartooth remains a dangerous wild force at DR1.

### Mission 7 — The Pay Convoy (triggered at Clock 6)
A convoy of wagons and Pitaxian guards (a Severe 11 encounter) appears on the
southwestern map edge. It travels 1 hex per day toward Fort Drelev and arrives on the
4th day. Scouting it reveals its route.
- **Intercepted:** +4 WC, and every **Mercenary** army gains 1 Doubt.
- **Arrives:** every Mercenary army loses all Doubt, and the Fort Guard recovers 1 HP.

### Mission 8 — A Flower Through the Ring (Fort Drelev, B8)
With Kisandra's jade ring (p. 299), slip into Fort Drelev using the module's
infiltration rules (p. 303) and meet **Satinder Morne**.
- **Reward:** +1 banked LP. From now on, Whispers Behind the Walls gets a +2 circumstance
  bonus and never takes the courier penalty. Satinder reveals the **secret escape tunnel**
  (B5, p. 301) for the final assault (§8).

### Mission 9 — Ameon's Last Contract (only if Ameon escaped Tatzlford)
Hunt Ameon Trask in the field. He moves with the Free Company and goes to ground in its
camp at night.
- **Reward:** if he is killed or captured, the Free Company becomes shaken 1 and
  gains 1 Doubt. A captured Ameon can be turned as described on p. 299.

### Mission 10 — Retake Tatzlford (only if Tatzlford was yours and fell)
As p. 299: storm the town or sneak in and remove Ameon (or his lieutenant), which makes
the occupying troops panic and disband.
- **Reward:** Tatzlford is Friendly again and Strain −2.

## 8. The Fall of Fort Drelev

The war is won when **Baron Drelev is defeated and the PCs reach 20 Liberation
Points**, exactly as Part 2 requires (p. 301). The campaign decides **how many LP the
PCs bring in** and **who is still defending the town**.

### Liberation Points from the campaign

At the start of the final approach, add up the banked LP (maximum 10):

- Missions 5 and 8, and Whispers Behind the Walls (maximum 6 from Whispers).
- **+1 LP for each Drelev army destroyed** during the campaign (maximum +4). Every
  dead mercenary is one less thug on the streets.
- Minus any LP lost to escalations (the bank can't go below 0).

Then play Part 2 as written, starting from that total.

### Fort Drelev's defenses

Use Appendix 3's fortification statistics:

| Fortification | Stats | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Drelev Keep** (B2) | Keep: AC 25, HP 5, up to 4 armies | The Fort Guard garrisons here. |
| **Watchtower** (B1) | Tower: AC 20, HP 2, 1 army | |
| **Town wall** | **Before Clock 16:** the gap makes it no better than a trench (AC 15, HP 1, 1 army). **From Clock 16:** Wooden Wall (AC 15, HP 2, 2 armies). | |

Fortified armies can't be engaged. To dig them out you need **siege engines**: a
mustered Siege army, the Stonethrowers or the Siege Battery turned with *Turn Their
Coin*, or the PCs opening the way from inside.

### Three ways in

**1. Infiltrate (the module's way).** The armies stay out of town or besiege it. The
PCs use p. 303's infiltration tactics, the secret tunnel if Satinder showed it
(Mission 8), and the banked LP. Each enemy army the campaign destroyed means fewer
patrols. If the **Fort Guard army was destroyed**, street patrols appear only half as
often (double the hours each infiltration tactic earns).

**2. Siege (starve them out).** One of your non-skirmisher armies takes the **Besiege**
order in the Fort Drelev hex.
- Each Night of siege, every enemy Mercenary army inside gains 1 Doubt, as unpaid
  sell-swords start slipping away. The town also suffers: apply the module's
  *Languishing* penalty (−2 LP per Night, to a minimum of 0).
- Drelev doesn't break from a siege alone. The PCs still have to go in after him, but
  with fewer defenders left.

**3. Storm (the Battle of Fort Drelev).** A **named battle**: a full War encounter
against every enemy army in the hex, with the fortifications above. **Strike the
Commander** means the PCs go into Drelev Keep after the baron while the armies fight
in the streets. Run the keep (areas C1–C51) as the PCs' half of the battle; the
**escape tunnel** puts them straight into the dungeons.
- **Victory:** Drelev's armies in town break. Bank +6 LP (+8 on a critical success
  when Determining Victory), even past the usual cap of 10. Street patrols end, so the
  PCs can move freely through the town.
- **Collateral:** each fortification your siege engines destroyed costs 2 LP (fires
  in the streets), applied as *Public Destruction*.
- **Loss:** your armies are routed from the hex, and the PCs inside the keep are on
  their own.

## 9. Ending the War

### Victory: Fort Drelev liberated
- Follow **Claiming Drelev** (p. 317). Drelev's hexes become unclaimed and can be
  annexed, and Fort Drelev becomes a freehold.
- **Campaign award:** 120 Kingdom XP (banked), Unrest −1d4, and one Ruin of the
  players' choice −1. If Strain ended **below 5**, also gain 1 Fame.
- Run the **Reckoning** (`rules.md` §11).

### Terms (Daily Event 17, or the PCs propose them)
At a parley, Drelev offers peace: the PCs **recognize his barony** and **cede every
Contested and enemy-held hex**, and he returns any prisoners.
- **Accept:** the war ends. Run the Reckoning, and every Contested hex is lost without
  a check. Fort Drelev can still be infiltrated later as in Part 2, but every escalation
  stays in force. Expect the PCs' honor and Kisandra's trust to suffer.
- **Stall:** a Statecraft check (Control DC) drags out the talks. On a success, War
  Clock −1. On a critical success, also reveal one enemy army. On a critical failure,
  the envoy storms off, and War Clock +2.
- **Counter-demand surrender:** Drelev refuses unless 3 or more of his armies are
  already destroyed. If they are, he flees to his keep, and the PCs can go straight to
  the Fall of Fort Drelev with War Clock −3.

### Armed Truce (War Clock 30)
Irovetti guarantees Drelev's throne. The war ends without a decision. Run the
Reckoning. Every Contested hex gets the normal check, and enemy-held hexes stay lost.
Drelev remains in power with every escalation in force, and Pitax is openly hostile.
That's a strong lead into Chapter 8.

### Collapse (Strain 20)
The kingdom's will breaks and the armies come home. Treat this as **Terms accepted**,
and the Collapse of Will crisis still applies.

## 10. Pacing Notes

- **Expected length:** 15–25 days. The War Clock alone reaches 30 by Day 30, and a
  good campaign pushes it back several times.
- **Day 1 should feel urgent:** raiders are loose in the Slough, the boggards the
  PCs gave away are guarding M'botuu against them, and the Outriders arrive at Dusk
  on Day 2. Give the players a clear first decision: protect the western farms, push a
  depot chain into the Narlmarches, or go personally to M'botuu to win back the
  boggards?
- **Rushing the fort is allowed but costly.** A party that goes straight for Fort
  Drelev on Day 3 faces every garrison, hostile boggards in the Slough, no banked
  LP, and raiders burning their depots behind them. That's a legitimate choice. Make the consequences visible.
- **Armies hold, heroes act.** The most memorable days will be the ones where the
  party leaves its armies dug in at Tok-Nikrat to walk into M'botuu and reclaim
  the boggards, or to burn the Forward Camp, and comes back to find out whether
  the line held.
- **The bridge is a side road.** Don't steer the players north. Let the Outriders'
  raids or Restov's relief column give them a reason, and let them decide if it's
  worth the detour.
